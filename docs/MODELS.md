# What the vendors document

Each model's licence, size, training cutoff and data, and the evaluations its own card reports, from primary
sources read on 2026-10-25. The machine-readable summary the report uses is `models.json`. A vendor's claim
is printed beside the benchmark's measurement, never in place of it.

**What the cards show, across all 22 models** (four added on 2026-10-25, below):
- **No card evaluates education or grading.** Gemma and Olmo list education as an intended use only.
- **Sycophancy** is reported by Anthropic only, qualitatively. Our pushback test measures it directly. Opus
  5.5 holds; Fable 5.1 and Haiku 5.5, whose cards say they improved, still give way by 3.5-3.7% of the scale.
- **Prompt injection** is reported by Anthropic and OpenAI for coding, browsing and tool use, not for
  instructions inside the work being judged. That is the gaming probe here.
- **Energy:** only Meta discloses training energy and carbon (Llama 3.2). No vendor discloses inference
  energy or water.
- **Training data** is described, not released, for every model on the board except Olmo 3.1 32B, added on
  2026-10-25, which releases all of it. Contamination can be checked against it directly once it is indexed
  (`contamination/`).
- **No document mentions student essays, grading data or education datasets as training data, or says it
  removed them.**
- **Removing benchmarks from training (decontamination)** is stated for six models: Phi-4 (13- and 7-gram
  matching against 20 named benchmarks), Olmo (against its whole evaluation suite), and Gemma 3 and 4
  (unnamed benchmarks and method). The other cards say nothing about it for training data.
- **Training on users' data:**
  - Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5: explicitly opt-in.
  - OpenAI's GPT-5 family and Gemini: stated, without saying whether it is opt-in.
  - DeepSeek: users' interactions, de-identified, with an opt-out (its EU training-content summaries for V4
    and V4.1); also employees' and partners' sessions, returned voluntarily.
  - Z.ai (GLM 5.3): only from users who opted in. GLM 5.3 Flash: not stated.
  - The rest: not stated.
- **Trained on other models' outputs:** Phi-4 (GPT-4o), Llama 3.2 (Llama 3.1), Ministral (Mistral Small and
  Medium), Qwen3 (Qwen2.5, QwQ), Olmo (GPT-4.1, QwQ, DeepSeek R1, Qwen3); DeepSeek and Gemma 3 name no
  teacher.
- **Transparency** (Stanford's Foundation Model Transparency Index, December 2025): Anthropic 46, Google
  41, OpenAI 35, DeepSeek 32, Meta 31, Alibaba 26, Mistral 18, against a mean of 40.69.

The survey below was compiled by an agent from the primary sources it lists, and spot-checked by the
maintainers' agent (the retention page, the Gemma 4 licence, the FMTI scores). Values marked
**unverified** could not be confirmed.

## Discrepancies and items not verified

- **Fable 5.1 prompt-injection ASR with probes.** Its own card says 12.80% (p.87). The Opus 5.5 card says 8.70% (p.88). The two cards may use different runs or configurations. Cite the card the comparison comes from.
- **Mistral default API retention (30 days).** Only secondary sources give it. The Mistral DPA mentions abuse monitoring but no period.
- **GPT-5.6 Luna.** The first-person-fairness value and the GPT-Red prompt-injection table appear only as a figure or table that did not render in text.
- **Not opened.** openai.com policy pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch. The OpenAI licence column rests on the existence of those policies, not on reading them.
- **Pages read from text conversions.** The Anthropic, OpenAI and Google cards were read as PDF/HTML-to-text conversions. Figure-only values were not read.
- **Education/grading evaluations.** None of the 17 cards, nor the Olmo report, reports an evaluation of education or grading use. Gemma 3/4 and Olmo list education only as an intended use.
- **Energy, carbon, water.** Only Meta (Llama 3.2) discloses training energy and carbon. OpenAI (gpt-oss), Microsoft (phi-4) and Ai2 (Olmo 3) disclose training compute only. No vendor discloses water or inference energy in these documents.

## Training data, in detail

Compiled 2026-10-25 from the documents listed in the survey above, plus the Gemma 3 and Gemma 4 technical reports and the Phi-4 data summary card. Each document was downloaded and searched in full text (keywords: training data, tokens, synthetic, user, personal/PII, filter, contamin/decontam, distill/teacher, language, educat/student/essay/grading).

**Conventions.** Page numbers are PDF page indices (as in the survey above; for the GPT-5 and gpt-oss PDFs the printed number is one lower). HTML cards (GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, Gemma 3/4 cards, Llama 3.2 card) are cited by section. "Not stated" means the document is silent after a full-text search. Nothing here is unverified: every source opened. Text-to-PDF conversions may miss figure-only content.

**Headline for a grading benchmark.** None of the 18 documents mentions student essays, grading data or education datasets as training data, or excludes them. Four documents state benchmark decontamination of training data, covering six of the 18 models: Phi-4 (benchmarks named, 13/7-gram), Olmo 3 (all OLMES benchmarks, n-gram), and the Gemma 3 and Gemma 4 reports (benchmarks not named). Qwen3 has a weak version that covers cold-start responses only. No vendor states decontamination against any grading or essay corpus (ASAP, Mohler, etc.). Closed-model cards (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) state no training-data decontamination at all.

### (b) Per-model notes

**Claude Opus 5.5** (system card, 22 Sep 2026). §1.1 p.11: "a proprietary mix of publicly available information from the internet, public and private datasets, user data included in feedback or bugs or which users have explicitly permitted for training" plus "synthetic data generated by other models". Filtering: "several data cleaning and filtering methods, including deduplication and classification" (p.11). ClaudeBot honours robots.txt; no password-protected or CAPTCHA pages. §1.2 p.11: crowd workers for "preference selection, safety evaluation, and adversarial testing". No token count, no language list, no decontamination of training data. Evaluation-side only: alignment evals aim "to minimize overlap with our training data" (p.92); DeepSWE tasks "written from scratch to avoid benchmark contamination" (p.175). p.123–124: prompt-injection training data came from a model prompted with a rubric whose transcripts were added to Opus 5.5's training data (the models are not named).

**Claude Fable 5.1** (joint card with Mythos 5.1, 1 Sep 2026). §1.1 p.11 lists "publicly available information from the internet, public and private datasets, and synthetic data generated by other models". **Unlike the Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 cards it does not mention user data.** The card is silent on user data, which is not the same as saying none was used. The filtering and ClaudeBot text are the same as Opus. Crowd workers §1.2 p.12. p.174: the June 2026 MathArena set may overlap training data; "some contamination cannot be ruled out".

**Claude Haiku 5.5** (condensed card, 7 Oct 2026). §1.1 p.8 has the same wording as Opus 5.5, including opt-in user data and synthetic data from other models. Crowd workers §1.2 p.8. Evaluation-side contamination notes only (p.14 unpublished bio tasks; p.26 novel cyber scenarios).

**GPT-6 Astra** (deployment safety card, 3 Sep 2026). §2 is the whole data statement: "trained on diverse datasets and filtered through our data processing pipeline, including to reduce personal information." Unlike the GPT-5 and GPT-5.6 cards it does not list users or human trainers. In the biological and chemical safety training section: "Training data includes synthetic, production, and semi-synthetic examples". "Production" implies deployment traffic, but the card does not say whether that use is opt-in or opt-out. Languages: the card points to the ChatGPT supported-languages list. The UK AISI notes a possible contamination issue in one of its own evals; that is not a training-data statement.

**GPT-5 Mini** (GPT-5 system card, 13 Aug 2025; the card covers gpt-5-thinking-mini alongside the other GPT-5 models). §2 PDF p.6: "information that is publicly available on the internet, information that we partner with third parties to access, and information that our users or human trainers and researchers provide or generate." It mentions "rigorous filtering to maintain data quality", "advanced data filtering processes to reduce personal information", and the Moderation API plus safety classifiers, including against CSAM. It says nothing about opt-in or opt-out, gives no token count, and states no decontamination. Languages appear only in evaluation: MMLU translated into 13 languages (PDF p.19–20).

**GPT-5.6 Luna** (GPT-5.6 card, Sol/Terra/Luna, 9 Jul 2026). §2 has the same sources wording as GPT-5, including users and human trainers. Filtering covers quality, personal information and safety classifiers. The bio safety training section uses the same "synthetic, production, and semi-synthetic examples" wording as Astra. There is nothing Luna-specific about data.

**GPT-OSS 20B** (gpt-oss model card, 5 Aug 2025). §2.4 PDF p.6: "a text-only dataset with trillions of tokens, with a focus on STEM, coding, and general knowledge". Pre-training was filtered for harmful content "by reusing the CBRN pre-training filters from GPT-4o". §2.5 PDF p.7: post-trained "using similar CoT RL techniques as OpenAI o3". The card does not call this distillation. No personal-information filtering, no languages and no decontamination are stated. MMLU appears in 14 languages as an evaluation only (PDF p.10).

**DeepSeek V4.1 Flash** (tech report on HF, undated; repo Sep 2026). The corpus is 45T multimodal tokens (p.1, p.22), with "a 7:1 token ratio of text-only to multimodal data" (p.21). Text curation (p.20–21): the report filters out "model-generated content with limited information gain" and "low-quality machine-translated text". It adds more recent open-source code and has domain experts define quality dimensions. Multimodal data was re-bootstrapped "from Common Crawl", alongside PDFs, domain datasets, image-code pairs and computer-use trajectories. RL environments (§5.1, p.26) use interaction data that "internal employees and external partners" return "on a voluntary basis", plus coding-agent sessions and starred GitHub repos. §5.2.4 p.31: final on-policy distillation "using over 40 teacher models" (not named; "may differ architecturally"). There is no mention of contamination, PII filtering or languages.

**Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite** (card, 21 Jul 2026). Its Training Dataset and Processing sections defer to the 3.1 Flash-Lite card, whose p.2 defers to Gemini 3 Pro. **Gemini 3 Pro card p.3** says the data includes "publicly available datasets", "data obtained by crawlers", "licensed data obtained via commercial licensing agreements", user data "pursuant to user controls, where appropriate", workforce data, and "AI-generated synthetic data". Post-training used instruction-tuning, RL and human-preference data. Processing covered deduplication, robots.txt, safety filtering, quality filtering, and filtering of pornographic, violent and CSAM content. It gives no personal-information filtering, no tokens, no languages and no decontamination. "Based on" is the only lineage statement, and the mechanism (distillation or fine-tune) is not given.

**Gemma 3 27B / 4B** (model card, 14 Aug 2025; Gemma 3 technical report, arXiv 2503.19786, Mar 2025). Card: 27B "trained with 14 trillion tokens", 4B "4 trillion tokens". Sources are web documents ("over 140 languages"), code, mathematics and images. Filtering covers CSAM, "Sensitive Data Filtering" of "certain personal information", and quality and safety. Report §2 p.3: "We decontaminate evaluation sets from our pre-training data mixture" (no benchmarks or method named), plus a quality reweighing step. It describes pre-training "with knowledge distillation" (teacher unnamed) and post-training distillation "from a large IT teacher" (p.4), with reward models "trained with human feedback data". p.6 says that despite decontamination "there is always a risk of contamination".

**Gemma 4 26B A4B / 31B** (model card, 30 Jul 2026; Gemma 4 technical report, arXiv 2607.02770, 19 Jun 2026). Card: web documents ("over 140 languages"), code, mathematics, images, January 2025 cutoff; filtering is the same as Gemma 3. Report §2.4 p.3: "We filter data to decontaminate benchmarks", with no benchmarks named; pre-training is "similar" to Gemma 3. §3 p.5: post-training filters "certain personal information, unsafe or toxic model outputs". It gives no token count, and the word "distillation" does not appear in the report.

**Llama 3.2 3B** (MODEL_CARD.md). Training Data: "up to 9 trillion tokens of data from publicly available sources". The 1B/3B models "incorporated logits from the Llama 3.1 8B and 70B models" as token-level targets, and knowledge distillation was used after pruning. Fine-tuning data combined "human-generated data from our vendors with synthetic data". LLM-based classifiers select high-quality prompts. There are eight officially supported languages, "trained on a broader collection". It states no decontamination, PII filtering or user data.

**Phi-4** (technical report arXiv 2412.08905; HF card; data_summary_card.md). Tokens: the report says "approximately 10T tokens" (p.7) and the HF card says 9.8T. Mixture, Table 5 p.10: web 15%, web rewrites 15%, synthetic 40%, code 20%, acquired sources 10% ("academic data and books"). Sources include "licensed books", arXiv, PubMed Central and GitHub (p.6). Web seeds were filtered for "educational potential" (p.5), and "educational forums" are a named source (p.6). Decontamination, App. B.1 p.27: a "hybrid n-gram algorithm" with 13-gram and 7-gram features against ARC-Easy, MBPP, phibench, CommonsenseQA, WinoGrande, mcphi, MedQA, MATH, AGIEval, PIQA, OpenBookQA, HellaSwag, GPQA, mt-bench, MMLUPro, GSM8k, HumanEval, arena hard, ARC-Challenge and MMLU. The model was also tested on the fresh Nov 2024 AMC tests (p.3). Distillation: "its teacher GPT-4o" (p.2); synthetic-generation prompts were "provided to GPT-4o" (p.24); DPO responses came from GPT-4o and GPT-4t, with GPT-4o as judge (p.12). Multilingual data is about 8% (HF card), and SFT covered 40 languages (p.12). The data summary card answers the personal-data question only with "Microsoft follows all relevant laws and regulations" (§2.3). It confirms commercial licensing agreements (§2.2.1).

**Ministral 8B** (Ministral 3 paper, arXiv 2601.08584, Jan 2026; HF card). p.1: "trained for between 1 and 3 trillion tokens by leveraging Mistral Small 3.1". Pretraining uses "Cascade Distillation" (prune, then distil from Mistral Small 3.1 logits) on "a mixture of text-only and interleaved text with image data" (p.4); the composition is not described. SFT is "distilled from Mistral Medium 3" (p.5). RL draws Q-A pairs "from a diverse set of open and proprietary sources" (p.5). The HF card lists 11 languages. It states no decontamination, PII filtering or user data.

**Qwen3 235B** (Qwen3 Technical Report, arXiv 2505.09388, May 2025; this report describes the original Qwen3-235B-A22B, not the 2507 update served on OpenRouter). §3.1 p.3–4: "119 languages and dialects, with a total of 36 trillion tokens". Text was OCR'd from PDFs with Qwen2.5-VL. Qwen2.5, Qwen2.5-Math and Qwen2.5-Coder "synthesize trillions of text tokens" (textbooks, QA, instructions, code); the share is not stated. An annotation system labelled "over 30 trillion tokens" by "educational value, fields, domains, and safety". §4.1 p.10: cold-start responses come from QwQ-32B; "human annotators manually assess" the cases it fails; responses "overly similar to potential validation set items" are removed. That is the only contamination-related step. Strong-to-weak distillation (§4.5 p.12) is for the smaller models. There is no PII filtering or user data.

**Olmo 3.1 32B Instruct** (Olmo 3 report, arXiv 2512.13961; data released on HF). Pretraining used Dolma 3 Mix, 5.9T tokens; the 32B run was truncated at 5.5T (p.11). Midtraining was 100B and long-context 100B (p.4). PDFs went through PII filtering (p.17), which removes documents that "link biographical, medical, location, employment, or educational information to a specific individual". Manual annotators set the PII taxonomy. Decontamination §3.5.3 p.26: the "decon" n-gram tool, "We decontaminate against all benchmarks in the OLMES package", focused on midtraining. Post-training (p.41) used the Tülu 3 procedure, "8-grams and an overlap threshold of 0.5". Instruct SFT (§5.2.2 p.56) draws chat prompts from WildChat, with older completions replaced by GPT-4.1. Tool trajectories came from GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-5 and GPT-4o/4.1/5 (p.55–56), including "consented, publicly released user prompts from SearchArena". DPO chose Qwen3 32B responses over Qwen3 0.6B ones (p.43–50). Table 30 p.58 lists all Instruct prompt sources (WildChat 302k, Aya, FLAN, Tülu 3 Persona, CoCoNot, WildGuardMix, WildJailbreak, OpenAssistant, …). Education: long-context synthetic rewrites by OLMo 2 Instruct include "a dialogue between a professor and student" and "a school quiz" formats (p.34). No student-work corpus is listed.
